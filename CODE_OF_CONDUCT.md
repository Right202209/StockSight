# Code of Conduct

## Our Pledge

We are committed to making participation in StockSight welcoming, respectful,
and harassment-free for everyone, regardless of age, body size, disability,
ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of
experience, education, socioeconomic status, nationality, personal appearance,
race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, healthy, and
welcoming community.

## Our Standards

Examples of behavior that contribute to a positive environment include:

- Demonstrating empathy and kindness toward other people.
- Being respectful of differing opinions, viewpoints, and experiences.
- Giving and accepting constructive feedback gracefully.
- Taking responsibility for mistakes and learning from them.
- Focusing on what is best for the community and the project.

Examples of unacceptable behavior include:

- Sexualized language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks.
- Public or private harassment, or publishing others' private information.
- Other conduct that could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a
  professional setting.

## Scope and Enforcement

This Code applies in project spaces and whenever an individual is officially
representing the project or its community. Project maintainers are responsible
for clarifying and enforcing these standards and may remove, edit, or reject
comments, commits, code, issues, or other contributions that violate this Code.
They may also take other appropriate action in response to unacceptable
behavior.

## Reporting

Please do not report conduct concerns in public issue threads. For a sensitive
report, use GitHub's private reporting channel for this repository when it is
available. If that channel is unavailable, open a minimal issue requesting a
private contact method and do not include confidential details.

Reports will be reviewed promptly and kept as confidential as practical. A
report should include the relevant project location, a description of what
happened, and any supporting context that can be shared safely.
